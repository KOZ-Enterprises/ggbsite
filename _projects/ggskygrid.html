ggSkyGrid · Learning autonomous robotics

From a rover to a coordinated drone swarm

My goal is to build robots that can understand where they are, move toward a goal and eventually work together. I’m starting with a ground rover, then one small indoor drone, before attempting coordinated flight with several aircraft.

Current focus: building the rover · Roadmap updated October 17, 2026

The goal

ggSkyGrid is my plan for learning autonomous robotics from the ground up: robots that work out where they are, choose a route and reach a goal without me steering every move.

The destination is a small group of drones flying planned formations, checking that they stay apart and with a way to stop one drone, or all of them, when something goes wrong. I’m getting there in steps: a ground rover first (easy to test, cheap to crash), then simulation, then one small indoor drone, and only then more drones.

The projects

ggSkyGrid is the umbrella over five projects. Three sit on the main path; the stages they appear in are the milestones in the roadmap below.

ggGridRunner

Ground rover

Stages 1–4 and 9–11

A rover with four steerable wheels. The goal is to go from reliable remote driving to sensing its own movement, building a map and driving itself to a destination.

Where it fits: where I learn motors, sensors and control on a vehicle that is safe to test.

Status: building now — motor tests, the radio link and the chassis.

ggChoreo

Planning software

Stages 5–6 and 12–13

Software that gives each drone a place in a formation, plans its route and checks that the routes stay apart and within motion limits.

Where it fits: turns a formation into movements I can check in simulation before anything flies.

Status: version 1 built; it plans, checks and replays routes in simulation.

ggSwarm

Drone coordination

Stages 7–8 and 12–13

A research project that began as a simulation capstone: several drones learning to work together. The goal is real coordinated flight, starting with planned routes and later adapting plans in flight.

Where it fits: brings planning, flight and group behavior together.

Status: simulation capstone complete. No physical drone swarm yet.

Optional side branches

Related interests, but neither is needed to finish the main path.

ggSkybit

Custom flight controller · Parked

A custom quadcopter for learning how flight electronics and control software keep an aircraft stable. The main path flies an existing indoor drone instead, so this build stays a separate experiment.

ggSkylight

Drone light shows · Not yet scoped

A possible future use for coordinated flight: outdoor drone light shows. The operating requirements and business scope are still open; the indoor work here is only an early foundation.
