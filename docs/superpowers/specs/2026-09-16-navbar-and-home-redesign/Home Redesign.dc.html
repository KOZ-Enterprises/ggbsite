GG Swarm

A decentralized coordination framework where global formation behavior emerges from local agent interactions — transitioning from Isaac Lab simulation to real-world drone light shows.

OBJECTIVE

Train graph-attention policies that let a drone swarm hold and morph formations without a central controller, then fly them on PX4 hardware.

The swarm treats each drone as a node in a dynamic graph. Neighbors within sensing range exchange state, a GATv2 encoder weighs those messages, and a PPO-trained policy turns them into velocity commands. No drone knows the global formation — it emerges.

Simulation runs in Isaac Lab with 512 parallel environments. The hardware path replaces the sim bridge with PX4 offboard control over MAVLink, which is where the interesting failures live: latency, dropped neighbors, and wind.

2026-05-01·6 MIN READ·GG SWARM

Introducing GG Swarm Live

The capstone outlived the semester. Here is what it takes to move GATv2 policies out of simulation and onto real airframes.

#ggswarm #robotics #reinforcement-learning

When the semester ended I had a working simulation and a deadline that no longer applied. The policies held formation across 30 evaluation episodes, which is a fine place to stop if the goal is a grade — and a terrible place to stop if the goal is drones in the air.

What sim gets wrong

Isaac Lab gives you perfect state at every timestep. Real neighbors drop out, arrive late, or report positions that disagree with yours. The policy never learned that the graph itself is unreliable.

  • Neighbor latency of 40–120ms over MAVLink, versus zero in sim.
  • Position estimates from GPS + optical flow, not ground truth.
  • Wind, which the sim models as nothing at all.
$ ros2 launch ggswarm offboard.launch.py agents:=4
[INFO] policy loaded: gatv2_ppo_512env_ep3200.pt
[WARN] neighbor 3 stale (112ms) — falling back to last known

The fix is not a better policy, it is training against the failure. Domain randomization over latency and dropout comes next, and the log will say whether it worked.

COURSEWORK/CST 363
CST 363 · SPRING 2025

Database Systems

Relational modeling, SQL, and the tradeoffs that push you toward a document store. The course project was a full-stack inventory app backed by MySQL, with a MongoDB rewrite to feel the difference.

What I took from it

Normalization is easy to recite and hard to hold onto once the queries get real. The useful lesson was watching a clean third-normal-form schema lose to a denormalized one on the read path, and having to justify the choice.

Coming from mechanical engineering, the transferable part was the modeling discipline: define the entities, define the relationships, and don't let the implementation talk you out of them.

About me

I'm an Environmental Control Systems (ECS) Analysis Engineer at Boeing and a recent Computer Science graduate from CSUMB. My route here wasn't a straight line. It ran through a BS and MS in Mechanical Engineering, a QC role certifying pipe fittings for nuclear submarines, seven years designing and testing flight hardware at Marvin Engineering, and now Boeing, where I investigate in-service ECS problems so the cabin stays comfortable, and breathable, at 35,000 feet.

The software half came later, and by choice. I failed a programming course in high school, decided it wasn't for me, and spent a decade as a mechanical engineer before teaching myself Python on the job and realizing I'd been wrong. These days I live where hardware meets software: mechanical systems running on intelligent code, heading toward autonomous drone swarms and distributed robotics.

My Journey

Two mechanical degrees gave me the physical side: how machines actually move, fail, and get tested. The CS degree added the other half — the algorithms, data, and code that make them coordinate. I can design the hardware, instrument the test, and write the software that runs it, which is exactly what building a drone swarm from scratch demands.

What I'm Building

I'm a firm believer in learning by building, breaking, and rebuilding. ggRover is my fun build: a semi-autonomous rover with an STM32 brain and a PS4 controller behind the wheel. GG Swarm started as my capstone project and outlived the semester — a GNN-based drone swarm design maturing from simulation toward hardware.
