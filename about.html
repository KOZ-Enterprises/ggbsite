Gary Kuepper // Long Beach, CA

About Me

I’m an Environmental Control Systems (ECS) Analysis Engineer at Boeing and a recent Computer Science graduate from CSUMB. My route here wasn’t a straight line. It ran through a BS and MS in Mechanical Engineering, a QC role certifying pipe fittings for nuclear submarines, seven years designing and testing flight hardware at Marvin Engineering, and now Boeing, where I investigate in-service ECS problems so the cabin stays comfortable, and breathable, at 35,000 feet.

The software half came later, and by choice. I failed a programming course in high school, decided it wasn’t for me, and spent a decade as a mechanical engineer before teaching myself Python on the job and realizing I’d been wrong. I went back for a BS in Computer Science at CSUMB, nights and weekends while working full-time, and graduated in April 2026. These days I live where hardware meets software: mechanical systems running on intelligent code, heading toward autonomous drone swarms and distributed robotics.

My Journey

Two mechanical degrees gave me the physical side, how machines actually move, fail, and get tested. The CS degree added the other half: the algorithms, data, and code that make them coordinate. That combination is the point. I can design the hardware, instrument the test, and write the software that runs it, which is exactly what building a drone swarm from scratch demands.

What I’m Building

I’m a firm believer in learning by building, breaking, and rebuilding. At Boeing, my day job is Environmental Control Systems (ECS) Analysis Engineer: resolving in-service safety problems to keep airplanes flying and everyone breathing onboard. Outside that, ggRover is my fun build, a semi-autonomous rover with an STM32 brain and a PS4 controller behind the wheel. GG Swarm started as my capstone project and outlived the semester: a GNN-based drone swarm design maturing from simulation toward hardware.
